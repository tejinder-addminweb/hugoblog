Ever wondered how to sell your car quickly and efficiently? Selling my F150 turned out to be a journey filled with twists and turns, and today, I’ll share my experience from start to finish. With an F150 Platinum in hand and a series of offers that left me scratching my head, I learned a lot about the car market and what it takes to get the right price for your vehicle.

## The Background of My F150

Let’s start with the truck itself. I purchased a 2021 F150 Platinum with a 6.7' bed from a Ford dealer in LA back in 2022 for a hefty $70,000. At the time, it was a significant investment, and I didn't even see the truck in person before buying it online. When I finally did pick it up, I noticed it was longer than other trucks I had seen, which felt a bit strange. 

When I bought it, the MSRP was around $78,000, and fast forward to today, that same truck is valued at about $85,000. Initially, the truck had around 14,000 miles on it. By the time I decided to sell, it had racked up 49,000 miles. With a 24K loan at 6.99% interest looming over me, I figured it was time to let it go, especially since I had another vehicle—a Cybertruck—that suited my urban lifestyle better.

## Modifications and Market Value

This F150 wasn’t just any truck; it was modified with some serious upgrades. I had installed Brixton Forged 23-inch forged wheels, an ARB Big Brake Kit, a CRF intercooler, and an AFE intake. I also tuned the engine, which meant it didn't pass California's emissions standards. 

Knowing the car market, I checked the MMR (Market Report) and saw that my truck should be worth around $44,000 wholesale. Feeling optimistic, I thought selling at that price would be a smooth process. However, reality hit hard when I started reaching out to dealers and getting offers.



![The Background of My F150 showing f150 implementation and best practices](https://asfeedqzyhobsvuctlxi.supabase.co/storage/v1/object/public/blog-images/f150-1-1761213519434.jpg)

## The Dealer Dilemma

I contacted several dealers I knew, including Carbecco and Space Auto. To my dismay, their offers were around $37,000, and they only wanted to consign it. I was frustrated, to say the least. I uploaded my truck's information to CarMax, Carvana, and AutoNation, but the highest offer I received was just $39,800.

Eventually, I decided to take my truck to Sunrise Ford for an evaluation. The used car manager was professional and friendly, and he came back with an offer of $41,700. Still, I felt it was too low, especially since he mentioned they wanted to keep their purchase $4,000 below MMR to reduce their risk.

## Auction Adventures

After that, I tried my luck with Cars & Bids, where I went through their entire process, including a thorough inspection from the Lemon Squad. The report came back great, but when the auction went live, I was disappointed to see that the final bid was only $40,250. It felt like a slap in the face after all that effort.

That’s when I remembered my previous success with BidBus, where I had sold a couple of high-end cars like a Porsche 911 and a Tesla Model S Plaid. Figuring it was my best shot, I decided to give it another go.

## The BidBus Experience

After uploading my truck to BidBus, I received an estimated value of around $44,000, which was on the high side. I had to wait for the auction to go live, which was scheduled for Monday, Wednesday, and Friday. Meanwhile, I received a tempting $45,000 offer from a VW dealer in Anaheim and went to check it out, feeling optimistic.

However, after a meticulous inspection that lasted three hours, they turned me down because of some oil residue on the rear differential. I was left frustrated. I immediately called BidBus to inform them of the situation.



![The BidBus Experience showing f150 implementation and best practices](https://asfeedqzyhobsvuctlxi.supabase.co/storage/v1/object/public/blog-images/f150-2-1761213552257.jpg)

## Finding a Buyer

The team at BidBus was incredibly helpful. They quickly contacted other bidders from the auction and lined up a Honda dealer who was willing to pay $44,000. I drove an hour to the Honda dealership, and to my surprise, they barely inspected the truck. They cut me a check for $44,000 right on the spot, and that was it. 

Interestingly, they had asked me for a new intake to meet smog requirements, which I quickly purchased on eBay for about $200 and sent to them. The loan on my F150 got paid off immediately, and I walked away feeling relieved.

## The Aftermath

Later, I discovered that the Honda dealer sold my truck for $46,000 retail. It’s interesting how the market works. My experience taught me the importance of understanding your vehicle's value and being patient during the selling process. 

If you’re in a similar position, whether you’re dealing with an old car, a dream car, or even a modified vehicle, know that the right platform can make a world of difference. 

## Key Takeaways

Selling my F150 through BidBus was a learning experience that reinforced several key points:

1. **Know Your Vehicle's Value**: Stay informed about the current market value for your vehicle. This will help you set realistic expectations.
   
2. **Be Prepared for Offers**: You may encounter offers that seem low, but don’t be discouraged. Keep exploring your options.
   
3. **Consider Auction Platforms**: If traditional dealerships aren’t meeting your expectations, online auction platforms like BidBus can connect you with buyers willing to pay more.

4. **Understand Modifications**: Be aware that modifications can impact your vehicle's resale value and marketability.

5. **Patience Pays Off**: Sometimes, the right buyer takes time to find. Don’t rush the process.

If you're looking to sell your car, consider using a platform like BidBus. It's essential to explore all avenues to get the most value for your vehicle.
