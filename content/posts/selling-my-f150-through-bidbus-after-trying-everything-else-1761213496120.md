Have you ever found yourself in a tough spot trying to sell a car? It can feel like a daunting task, especially when you think you’ve tried every option available. That's exactly where I found myself recently. I had a 2021 F150 Platinum that I bought online, and after a whirlwind of attempts to sell it, I finally turned to BidBus. Here’s my journey through selling that truck.

## The Background of My F150

Let’s rewind a bit. I bought my F150 from a Ford dealer in LA in 2022 for a whopping $70,000. Back then, the MSRP was around $78,000, but the market had only gotten crazier since then. Fast forward to today, I find myself with a truck that had around 14,000 miles when purchased, and now has about 49,000 miles. 

Why was I selling? Well, I was leaving the country for a while and didn't feel the need to keep two trucks. I already had my eyes set on a Cybertruck, and with a $24,000 loan at 6.99%, it was time to let my F150 go.

## The Modifications and Initial Offers

This was no ordinary truck. I had made several modifications including a Brixton Forged 23-inch wheel, an ARB Big Brake Kit, a CRF intercooler, and an AFE intake. The problem? The tune on the truck didn’t pass CARB regulations, which complicated things a bit.

I started my selling journey by checking the Manheim Market Report (MMR). The wholesale price was around $44,000 to $45,000, and I thought that was a fair price. However, when I reached out to dealers like Carbecco and Space Auto, the offers ranged from $37,000 to $39,800. I couldn’t believe it. 

So, I turned to CarMax, Carvana, and AutoNation. The offers were still underwhelming, hovering around the $40,000 mark. Finally, I went to Sunrise Ford, where I typically got my service done. They offered me $41,700, which was the best offer at that point, but it still felt low. The used car manager explained they wanted to keep their purchase about $4,000 below MMR to mitigate their risk.

## Exploring Online Auction Options

Desperate to sell, I decided to try Cars & Bids. I went through their whole Lemon Squad inspection process, which took about two weeks. They finally approved the listing, and the auction went live. Unfortunately, I quickly realized that while there were many inquiries, few were serious buyers. The final bid ended up at a disappointing $40,250. 

Feeling frustrated, I thought back to my previous experiences. I had successfully sold cars like a 911 and a Tesla Model S on BidBus. Maybe it was time to give that platform another shot.

## The BidBus Experience

On BidBus, I was given an estimate of around $44,000, which sounded promising. They only host auctions on Mondays, Wednesdays, and Fridays, so I had to wait a bit for it to go live. Meanwhile, I received a $45,000 offer from a VW dealer. Curious about why a VW dealer would want a truck, I decided to check it out.

When I arrived, the inspection took about three hours, and to my shock, they backed out due to some oil residue on the rear differential. At that point, I was feeling the pressure. What was I supposed to do now?

## A Quick Call to Action

I quickly called BidBus and explained the situation. They jumped right on it, contacting other bidders from the auction. They lined up a Honda dealer who was willing to offer me $44,000. I drove an hour to the Honda dealership, and honestly, they barely even looked at the truck. They handed me a check, and just like that, the deal was done.

I had to buy a new intake to pass smog regulations, but that was a small price to pay. I found one on eBay for about $200 and mailed it to them. My loan was paid off immediately, and I felt a huge weight lifted off my shoulders.

## The Aftermath

Later on, I discovered that the Honda dealer sold my old truck for $46,000 retail. It’s interesting to note how the market values can shift, especially with modified cars. 

This experience taught me a lot about the car market and the various avenues you can take when selling a vehicle. It’s essential to know your worth and be persistent. Just because a dealer throws out a low offer doesn’t mean it’s the best you can get.

## What This Means for You

If you’re also in the market for selling an old car, my journey highlights a few key takeaways:

- **Research Your Vehicle's Worth**: Always check reliable sources like the MMR to get an idea of your car's market value.
  
- **Explore Multiple Channels**: Don’t just settle for the first few offers. Try different dealers, online auctions, and platforms like BidBus to find a better deal.

- **Be Prepared for Inspections**: Ensure your vehicle is in good condition before selling. Small issues can lead to big losses in offers.

- **Stay Open to Offers**: Sometimes, the best offers come from unexpected places. Be flexible and ready to negotiate.

## Key Takeaways

Selling my F150 was a rollercoaster ride, but I managed to navigate through the ups and downs successfully. Whether you're selling an old car or a dream car, remember to stay informed, persistent, and open-minded.
